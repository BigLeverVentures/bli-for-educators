# Adapt a lesson for your learners

[Home](../README.md) · [Approach](approach.md) · [Authoring kit](authoring-kit.md)

Start with one task your learners actually need to do. You can use a lesson unchanged, adapt it for a different audience, or borrow its teaching method. You do not need separate permission, membership, Git knowledge or our platform to adapt and share our original educational material for teaching under the [Big Lever Educator Teaching-Use License 1.0](../LICENSE.md). Normal paid teaching and tuition, including sharing materials with enrolled learners, are allowed; repackaging for sale or charging specifically for access to covered materials is not. Prior CC BY rights in unchanged material remain valid regardless of which copy is used; see the license.

## 1. Choose a useful first lesson

| Your learners need to… | Start here | What they need |
|---|---|---|
| Try AI for the first time | [Get Your First Useful Result](../basics/basics-03.md) | A chat assistant; [account setup](../basics/basics-02.md) if needed |
| Write a recurring message | [Email Template Library](../exercises/email-template-library.md) | One familiar situation and its facts; a text chat is enough |
| Work through an unclear task | [Work Through a Problem with AI](../basics/basics-05.md) | A question or goal; no files or Project required |
| Rehearse an interview | [Interview Coach](../exercises/interview-coach.md) | Role and experience facts, or fictional practice cards; text chat is enough |

These are suggested starting points, not a required sequence. Use the [full catalog](../curriculum.md) for another goal. Technical and research tasks may need additional tools: try those tools yourself before promising the activity to learners.

Compare two complete adaptations, each with educator notes, a learner handout and trial checks:

- [Volunteer emails](volunteer-email-example.md) turns Email Template Library into a community-workshop activity that produces a reusable message.
- [First customer-service interview](interview-coach-example.md) turns Interview Coach into first-job role-play with two fictional applicants. It demonstrates rehearsal, feedback, learner choice and another attempt, and includes checks for your own teaching trial.

The people and facts are fictional. These examples demonstrate adaptation; neither has been tested in a classroom trial.

## 2. Make your own editable copy

Open a lesson from the [browser catalog](https://exercises.bigleverinstitute.org/). Select its title and lesson body, copy, and paste into a document you can edit. Include complete prompts and relevant help links. The **Copy this prompt** buttons copy individual prompts only. Copying rendered text avoids needing to edit HTML or Markdown code.

If you prefer GitHub, the lesson's `.md` page has a rendered Preview; copy that text rather than its source code. A GitHub account is unnecessary for reading and copying. Or start with the [volunteer-email handout](volunteer-email-example.md#learner-handout-start) or the [interview handout](interview-coach-example.md#learner-handout-start).

## 3. Change the situation, preserve the learning

Replace the audience, examples, vocabulary, inputs and useful result to fit your group. Translate, shorten or rearrange the material as needed. Delete references to features you will not use. Keep any required basic-skill links, or supply equivalent instructions in your handout.

Preserve these functions, even if you change all the wording:

- A recognizable job and a clear first action.
- Enough facts to do it, with a fictional option when private material is unsuitable.
- A complete prompt or guided conversation; learners can ask the assistant for explanations and help forming their request.
- Checks specific to the task, followed by the learner's choice of corrections.
- A usable result they can save and use again. Check consequential revisions against facts or actual behavior, not just the assistant's approval.

For the volunteer adaptation, we keep reusable fields and checks for invented commitments. We narrow the task to recruiting a volunteer, remove optional email-app connections, and add fictional facts plus a second case with changed details. The value is practice checking and reusing a message accurately, not a claim that a long prompt writes better than “draft this email.”

When changing the case, check the **whole assembled activity**, not just the new facts. A second interview card for Lee still conflicts with a prompt opening “I will role-play Sam.” Check names and assumptions in opening instructions, every copied prompt, feedback questions and saved-note labels. Refer to “the applicant in the card” where a fixed name is unnecessary. For a beginner, supply a complete second prompt if assembling fragments would be harder than copying it. Keep that teaching copy consistent with your editable master.

## 4. Try it before sharing it

Work through your complete handout as a learner, using only the instructions and linked help. Does copying work? Are all fields explained? Can you save the result without special tools? Check a normal case, missing information and a second use with changed facts. Assemble that second case exactly as the learner is told to; inspect the resulting messages and follow-up directions for conflicting or leftover facts. Deliberately insert one consequential error into a sample result and see whether the check helps someone find it. A fabricated result used for this test must be labeled as such.

For an email, check recipient, dates, requested action and unsupported promises. For code, run it on safe test data. For research, open the cited sources. Fix problems and repeat the affected steps. If the needed tool or evidence is unavailable, change the task or state what remains untested.

Then invite a willing colleague unfamiliar with the lesson to try it with safe material. Ask: “Where did you stop? What did you keep? Can you use it with different facts?” Note any help you gave. Fix the instructions rather than relying on explaining them aloud every time. If nobody has tried it yet, say so; you can invite a small pilot without claiming it is validated. [Evaluation and limits](evaluation.md) explains broader comparisons when you need stronger evidence.

## 5. Credit, share and improve

Keep one editable master of your adaptation. Share a document, PDF, workshop handout or page on your existing site; check the shared copy in a signed-out browser or as the intended learner. Keep prompts selectable, headings readable and required links accessible. You do not need to install our website or verification tools to teach one lesson.

Include attribution near the end. For example:

```text
Adapted from “Email Template Library,” Big Lever Institute for Educators:
https://github.com/BigLeverVentures/bli-for-educators/blob/main/exercises/email-template-library.md
Big Lever Educator Teaching-Use License 1.0:
https://github.com/BigLeverVentures/bli-for-educators/blob/main/LICENSE.md
Adapt and share for teaching; no resale of covered materials.
Prior CC BY rights in unchanged material remain valid regardless of the copy used.
Changes: adapted for volunteer coordination; added fictional examples;
removed email-app setup. Adapted by [YOUR NAME OR ORGANIZATION], [DATE].
```

Replace the bracketed fields. Use your own name and branding; do not imply BLI endorses your adaptation. See the license for third-party material and name/logo limits.

Add your own feedback contact and a plain status such as “Pilot version; reviewed by the author, not yet tried with learners.” Ask where people got stuck and what helped. Update your master and shared copy together. To tell BLI about an issue, use [GitHub issues](https://github.com/BigLeverVentures/bli-for-educators/issues) or help@bigleverinstitute.org; send only fictional or redacted examples.
