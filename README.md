# Big Lever Institute Curriculum for Educators

Practical AI lessons and resources for adapting them to your learners. For educators, nonprofits and community organizers who want to help people do useful work with AI.

Big Lever Institute is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. Our programming is always free. You can read, copy and adapt these lessons without joining our community or buying a course. Use any suitable AI assistant; individual tool tasks explain the capabilities they need.

## Start with your goal

- **Adapt your first lesson:** follow [Adapt a lesson for your learners](educators/adapt-a-lesson.md), then compare the complete [volunteer-email](educators/volunteer-email-example.md) and [first-job interview](educators/interview-coach-example.md) examples. No Git or publishing software is required.
- **Read lessons in your browser:** open the [standalone lesson catalog](https://exercises.bigleverinstitute.org/). No GitHub or community account is needed to read it.
- **Build or improve a learning program:** read [our approach](educators/approach.md), then use the [authoring kit](educators/authoring-kit.md).
- **Choose material for your learners:** browse the [curriculum and exercise library](curriculum.md).
- **Try an adaptation responsibly:** use [evaluation and limits](educators/evaluation.md) for a small trial in your own setting.
- **Try AI yourself:** begin with [AI Basics](basics/README.md). No AI account or prior AI knowledge is assumed at the beginning. Ordinary browser and typing familiarity helps.
- **Adapt or contribute:** read [reuse permissions](LICENSE.md) and [contribution guidance](CONTRIBUTING.md).

## What makes this approach worth sharing

A learner brings a real task, works through it in conversation, checks the result, chooses corrections and keeps something useful. A polished first draft is only one part of learning. We teach how to supply evidence, notice unsupported claims and make decisions about what to do next.

Our lessons must offer something worth the learner's effort beyond a competent ordinary question to an assistant. That is an editorial requirement, not a claim that our prompts always outperform shorter requests. Use [a fair comparison](educators/evaluation.md) to investigate whether your adaptation contributes useful learning.

This is an alpha release. Independent human learning effectiveness has not been established. Please try the material with your own audience and tell us where it helps or fails.
